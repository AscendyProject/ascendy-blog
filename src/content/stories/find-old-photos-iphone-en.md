If you've ever tried to find one photo from three years ago by scrolling up through your
library, you didn't have to. The fastest route depends on what you actually remember.

What follows is based on iOS 26. View names may differ slightly on other versions.

## If you roughly know when — Years and Months

This is the fastest one.

1. Open Photos and go to **Library**
2. Scroll the photo grid and buttons appear to switch to **Years** and **Months**
3. Switch to **Years** to browse year by year. Tap the year, tap the month, tap the day

"Sometime in the summer of 2023" is a few taps away. That's a completely different route
from dragging years of photos past your thumb.

## If you don't know when — Search

In iOS 26, Search is its own button at the bottom of the Photos app.

What search handles well:

- **People and pets** — if you've named them in Photos
- **Objects and scenes** — words like "snow", "cake", "beach"
- **Place names** — for photos that kept their location data

One thing worth knowing. **Searching in full sentences (like "cat in the garden") requires
Apple Intelligence, which needs an iPhone 15 Pro, an iPhone 16, or later.** On older
devices you get keyword search only. That's usually the reason search feels weaker than
you expected.

## If you only remember where — the map

"I know where we were, I just don't know when" is a common version of this problem.
For that, the map beats the calendar.

Inside the **Collections** tab there's a view that pins your photos on a map. Zoom into the
area and you get only the photos taken there. For travel photos this route is usually
the fastest.

## If there was text in the photo — search from the Home Screen

Receipts, signs, tickets, screenshots — if the photo has **text in it**, you can find it by
that text. The most reliable route is to **swipe down on the Home Screen** to open search
and type a word you remember; photos containing that text come up. It sometimes works from
Search inside Photos too, so both are worth trying.

If you're the kind of person who screenshots tickets and booking confirmations before a
trip, this is the most useful method on this list. Same for the photo you took of where you
parked.

## If you remember it as a trip — Collections

The **Collections** tab holds groupings the phone builds on its own: Trips, Memories,
People & Pets, Recent Days. "That trip three years ago" is often already bundled as one
thing, so you can open the whole trip at once.

## Four cases where none of this works

That's as far as the phone takes you. The next four aren't about not knowing the trick.
They're things the current structure doesn't do well.

**1. When two conditions overlap.** "Out of the beach photos with the baby, only the good
ones." You can filter by person, and you can filter by place, but stacking them to narrow
down is hard. And "good ones" isn't a search term at all.

**2. When the memory is vague.** "Some beach, summer, two years ago?" If you're not sure of
the year, the Years view loses its advantage and you're scrolling again.

**3. When you find them and there are too many.** Search your child's name and 3,000 photos
come back. That isn't finding. The picking is still yours to do.

**4. When the photo's date is wrong.** This one is hard to diagnose, so it eats time.
Photos received through messaging apps, or transferred from a phone you used before,
**sometimes arrive without their original capture date.** Those can end up sorted under the
day you received them. If a photo taken in 2020 sits in 2024, the Years view won't turn it
up. For those, search by **place or by text in the photo** instead of by date.

## Summary

| What you remember | Fastest route |
|---|---|
| Roughly the date | Library → Years → Months |
| A person or an object | Search |
| The place | The map inside Collections |
| Text in the photo | Swipe down on the Home Screen |
| That it was a trip | Trips and Memories in Collections |
| Nothing for certain | Start with the map. The date may be wrong |
