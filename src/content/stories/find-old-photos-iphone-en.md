"Where did that photo go."

What happens next is usually the same. You open Photos and start dragging your thumb
upward. Last year goes by, the year before that goes by, you accidentally tap and a random
photo opens full screen, you close it, you lose your place, you start over. And then you
give up.

It isn't that the photo is gone. It's right there. You just took the route nobody should
have to take.

What follows is based on iOS 26; view names may differ slightly on other versions. You also
don't need to read it in order — **what you happen to remember** decides the route, so skip
to your case.

## "It was around the summer of..." — when you half-remember the date

This is the easiest case. It's also the one people most often solve with their thumb.

Open **Library** in Photos and scroll the grid. Buttons appear to switch to **Years** and
**Months**. Switch to Years to browse year by year: tap the year, tap the month, tap the
day.

"Sometime in the summer of 2023" is a few taps away. That's an entirely different route
from dragging years of photos past your thumb. If you remember even roughly when, this is
the shortest way in.

## "No idea when. I just want that photo." — Search

Plenty of times the date is simply gone. In iOS 26, Search sits at the bottom of the Photos
app as its own button.

What search handles well:

- **People and pets** — if you've named them in Photos
- **Objects and scenes** — words like "snow", "cake", "beach"
- **Place names** — for photos that kept their location data

Here's where people get the wrong impression. Searching in full sentences (like "cat in the
garden") only works if **Apple Intelligence is available to you.** That comes with
conditions: an iPhone 15 Pro or 15 Pro Max, or an iPhone 16 or later; iOS 18.1 or newer;
Apple Intelligence switched on in Settings; and your device and Siri language set to a
supported language.

Without that, you're working with the keyword search described above. If search behaves
differently than you expected, check this first — often the feature isn't missing, the
conditions just weren't met.

## "I know exactly where we were" — the map

This one comes up more than you'd think. The date is a blur, but the place is sharp. That
beach, that café, that hospital.

When that's the case, the map beats the calendar. In **Library**, tap the menu button and
choose **Show Map**. Your photos appear spread across a map; zoom into the area and you get
only the ones taken there. Worth knowing: only photos that kept their location data show
up.

For travel photos this route is usually the fastest. The place tends to outlast the date in
memory.

## "There was text in it" — receipts, tickets, screenshots

If you're the kind of person who screenshots booking confirmations before a trip,
photographs the parking level, or keeps receipts as photos, this is the most useful method
here.

When a photo has text in it, you can find it by that text. There are two routes: type the
word into **Search** inside Photos, or **swipe down on the Home Screen** and type it there.
Both are meant to find text inside images, so if one comes up empty, try the other.

## "I want that whole trip" — Collections

Sometimes you don't want one photo. You want those few days back.

The **Collections** tab holds groupings the phone builds on its own: Trips, Memories,
People & Pets, Recent Days. "That trip three years ago" is often already bundled as one
thing, so you can open the whole trip at once.

## Four cases where you'll still end up doing it by hand

Even knowing everything above, these four are where searching stops and hand-work starts.
How much they bite depends on your device, your settings, and the photos themselves.

**One. Conditions stack, and one of them lives in your head.** "Out of the beach photos
with the baby, only the good ones." On a device with Apple Intelligence you can ask for the
first part in a sentence. What catches is **"good ones"** — not blurry, eyes open, a decent
expression. That's my standard, not a property attached to the photo. This is where I end up
scrolling the results and picking by hand.

**Two. The memory is vague.** "Some beach, summer, two years ago?" If you're not sure of the
year, the Years view loses its advantage and you're back to scrolling.

**Three. You find them, and there are too many.** Search your child's name and years of
photos come back at once. That isn't finding. The picking is still yours.

**Four. The photo's date is wrong.** This one is hard to diagnose, so it eats time. Photos
received through messaging apps, or transferred from a phone you used before, **sometimes
arrive without their original capture date.** Those can end up sorted under the day you
received them. If a photo taken in 2020 sits in 2024, no amount of digging in the Years
view will turn it up.

The first thing to try is the sort order. iOS 26 lets you sort the library by **date added**
or by **date taken** (Sort and Filter). If the capture date survived, switching that alone
puts the photo back where it belongs. If it didn't, search by place or by text in the photo
instead of by date.

## At a glance

| What you remember | Fastest route |
|---|---|
| Roughly when | Library → Years → Months |
| A person or an object | Search |
| The place | Library → menu → Show Map |
| Text in the photo | Search in Photos, or swipe down on the Home Screen |
| That it was a trip | Trips and Memories in Collections |
| Nothing for certain | Start with the map. The date may be wrong |
